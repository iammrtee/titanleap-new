Our read: Relaydesk's product isn't the problem. People who try it like it. The problem is that most visitors never get to try it: the pricing page gives them nothing to compare, the trial asks for a card before they've seen anything, and the headline doesn't say what they get.

No annual option. No bigger plan. $49 feels expensive with nothing next to it.

What's wrong

Visitors see one price and have to decide yes or no. With nothing to compare it to, most say "later". Relaydesk's pricing page has the highest exit rate on the site: 71% of visitors leave from it.

The fix

  • Add yearly billing with two months free. Show the yearly price first.
  • Add a "Business" plan at $89 a seat with SSO and priority support. Few will buy it, but it makes $49 look like the sensible choice.
  • Label Team as "Most popular".

How you'll know it worked

Pricing page exit rate drops below 60% within two weeks, and trial starts from the pricing page go up.

What's wrong

Of the people who click "Start free trial", 58% leave at the card form. They haven't seen a single thing Relaydesk does yet, so there's no reason to trust it with a card.

The fix

  • Remove the card fields from signup. Ask for email and team name only.
  • Ask for the card on day 7, inside the app, right after they've answered their 10th ticket.
  • Send one email on day 5: "Your team answered 43 tickets this week. Keep going for $49 a seat."

How you'll know it worked

Trial starts roughly double. Trial-to-paid dips at first, then settles above today's 11% as more real teams get in.

Where Relaydesk's next customers will come from

Once the leaks are fixed, more traffic is worth paying for. These two channels fit who buys Relaydesk: online stores with 5 to 50 staff who answer support email themselves.

Channel 1

Cold email to growing online stores

Shopify stores listing a support@ address, with 5 to 50 staff. They're big enough to feel the pain and small enough that the founder reads their email.

First step this week: build a list of 200 stores and send 20 personal emails a day that open with the store's slowest reply time you could measure.
Channel 2

"Zendesk alternative" searches

People searching for alternatives already know they need a help desk and are unhappy with their current one. They're the closest thing to a ready buyer.

First step this week: publish one honest comparison page, "Relaydesk vs Zendesk for small teams", with a real price comparison at 5 seats.

The biggest content leak: Relaydesk posts about twice a week and gets decent engagement, but only 1 in 12 posts tells people what to do next, and there's no link in the bio. Their best post (a reel on "why customers stop replying") got 4 times their average engagement and sent nobody anywhere. Fix: put the free trial link in the bio and end every post with one clear next step.

The uncomfortable truth

Relaydesk has been buying ads to get more traffic. The traffic isn't the problem. Every extra visitor is going through the same three leaks, so the ads are mostly paying to show people a pricing page they'll leave. Pause any spend increase until fixes 1 and 2 are live.
